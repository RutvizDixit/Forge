HOW FORGE WORKS

Not just an answer. Show me why.

FORGE is a product-intelligence workspace for turning scattered technical product information into decisions that can be reviewed, compared and traced back to their sources.

Guided explanation
01
THE BASIC IDEA

FORGE sits between product information and the decision.

Suppose someone gives you a product catalogue containing hundreds of components and asks:

“Which product should I use for this requirement?”

Searching the catalogue is only the beginning. You still need to understand the specifications, compare units, check whether the product actually satisfies the requirement and determine whether the information is reliable enough to support the decision.

That is the problem FORGE is designed around.

INPUTS

Give FORGE the information you already have.

Product information rarely arrives in one perfectly structured database. It may be spread across a catalogue, a technical PDF, a manufacturer's document and a web page.

i
The source stays important.

FORGE does not treat uploaded information as anonymous text. Source identity and provenance are preserved so a result can be investigated later.

03
PROCESSING

Uploading a file is only the first step.

1

Read the source

FORGE identifies useful content inside the supplied file or source. Depending on the format, that may include rows, fields, paragraphs, tables or structured records.

2

Identify product information

Product names, manufacturers, part numbers, specifications and related fields are organized into records that can be worked with.

3

Normalize comparable values

The same physical or technical quantity may appear using different units or notation. FORGE creates comparable representations while keeping the original value available.

4

Preserve provenance

Information remains associated with its source so that a later result can be traced back to where the information originated.

04
REQUIREMENT MATCHING

“Match” does not mean “the product sounds relevant.”

When you enter a requirement, FORGE evaluates the available product information against that requirement.

✓

Supported

The available information provides support for the requirement.

?

Needs review

The source does not provide enough information to confidently conclude that the requirement is satisfied.

×

Not supported

Available information conflicts with the requirement.

!
Missing information is not a failure.

If a product datasheet does not mention a specification, FORGE should not automatically claim that the product lacks it. The correct state may be uncertainty or a request for review.

05
EVIDENCE

Every important conclusion should have something behind it.

Evidence is the information that supports a conclusion. It can be an extracted value from a catalogue row, a specification in a technical document or another identifiable piece of source information.

07
OPTIONAL SEMANTIC REASONING

AI can help interpret information. It should not invent evidence.

Some product requirements are easy to check deterministically. Others involve language, terminology or context where semantic reasoning can be useful.

FORGE can use an optional language-model layer for those cases. The model can help interpret ambiguous text, summarize information or assist with qualitative reasoning.

USEFUL FOR
  • Interpreting technical language
  • Understanding similar terminology
  • Summarizing source material
  • Assisting qualitative comparison
NOT A SUBSTITUTE FOR
  • Missing source evidence
  • Explicit requirement checks
  • Actual product specifications
  • Human review where information remains uncertain

From a catalogue to a defensible product choice.

You receive a catalogue

“0–10 bar, 24 V supply, 4–20 mA output.”

04

05

Their specifications and meaningful differences are shown side by side.

07

The result is supported by an inspectable information path rather than a black-box claim.

09
IMPORTANT LIMITS

What FORGE will not pretend to know.

01 A missing specification

Missing data remains missing. It should not be silently converted into a negative conclusion.

02 An unsupported claim

A generated explanation does not become evidence merely because it sounds technically convincing.

03 A perfect decision

FORGE helps structure and inspect the decision. The final engineering or purchasing judgment remains with the responsible person.

04 A source it never received

FORGE can only reason from information available to it. It should never imply that it checked a document or specification that was not supplied.
